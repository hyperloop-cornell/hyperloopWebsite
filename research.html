Engineering Design CH-EDD-26

Engineering Design Document

The 2025–26 design cycle moved the pod away from heavy, off-the-shelf industrial hardware toward a custom-engineered, modular system that is lighter, smaller, and easier to build.

The document walks through all eight subsystems: a press-fit aluminum chassis, switchable magnetic and dual-sided pneumatic brakes, a parametrically optimized linear induction motor, custom levitation electromagnets, in-house power electronics, and a cloud-connected command-and-control stack.

02
Technical Aspects · Controls CH-TA-26

Dynamics-Aware Control of Electromagnetic Levitation

for Hyperloop Pods Under Motion, Track Irregularity, and Transient Loading

Maglev pods are usually sized with a static force balance: magnetic lift equals pod weight. This paper argues that is where levitation design starts, not where it ends.

It builds a reduced-order model of a pod with front and rear levitation modules, coupling nonlinear magnetic force, finite coil-current dynamics, heave and pitch, speed-dependent track irregularity, eddy-current effects, and braking load transfer. Three controllers are then compared across the resulting operating envelope.

Static lift calculations can start the design, but they cannot finish it.

From the conclusion
  1. R1
    Hover is a weak testOn a flat guideway, every controller meets the static lift condition and holds the design gap. The differences only appear in motion.
  2. R2
    Speed turns track error into frequencyA guideway error of wavelength λ reaches the controller at f = v/λ. At speed, the same bump pushes into the actuator's bandwidth.
  3. R3
    Braking over a defect is the critical caseLoad transfer, pitch coupling, finite current response, and a local gap disturbance all arrive at once.
  4. R4
    The safe envelope is the metricStatic sizing sets the operating point. Closed-loop simulation sets the safe speed and disturbance envelope around it.

Controllers Compared
CTL-01

PID + Feedforward

A static feedforward current from the nominal force balance, corrected by local gap feedback at each module. Simple to validate and adequate near the operating point.

CTL-02

Coordinated Body Feedback

State feedback on heave and pitch together, so front and rear modules act as one body. Gains can be scheduled across speed, acceleration, and payload.

CTL-03

Constrained Nonlinear

Regulates the instantaneous gaps, allocates feasible module forces, and adds a soft clearance barrier near the hard gap. It earns its complexity once actuator limits become active.

Socio-Economic Aspects CH-SE-26

Hyperloop on the Amsterdam–Berlin Corridor

Can hyperloop outperform rail and aviation on a real, high-demand European route? This study tests the question on the ~575 km Amsterdam–Berlin corridor.

It combines a three-phase kinematic travel-time model, a comparative lifecycle-emissions assessment across hyperloop, rail, and aviation, and EU infrastructure cost benchmarks, then examines the conditions under which hyperloop could meaningfully help decarbonize intercity travel.

TIME

A same-day round trip

Sub-hour travel holds even at a gentler 0.3 g, which adds only 1.7 minutes. The real threat is terminal time: security and boarding have to stay within 10–15 minutes.

CARBON

Cleaner as the grid gets cleaner

Hyperloop already beats aviation on today's grid. Germany's energy policy and early occupancy (below ~50%, per-passenger emissions climb fast) are the swing variables.

COST

Tunnels drive the budget

Elevated track over flat terrain runs EUR 25–35M per km. The 40–60 km of urban tunnels add EUR 2.8–4.2B, with ~EUR 45M a year in maintenance.
